🏪 Small-business buying guide

Choose the assistant your business can actually maintain.

A website assistant can help visitors find answers, but a small business does not need another opaque system to babysit. Start with a narrow job, a reliable source, and a visible route to a person.

Choose one customer job

Start with documented questions such as services, opening hours, delivery areas, policies, or booking steps. Avoid promising that the assistant can replace every support or sales conversation.

Make the source trustworthy

Remove stale prices, duplicate pages, and contradictory policies before connecting the assistant. Assign one person to review the source when the business changes.

Test the “I don’t know” path

Ask an undocumented question, a sensitive question, and a question with two possible interpretations. The useful response is a clear boundary and a human handoff—not a confident guess.

Keep contact visible

Show a phone number, email, booking route, or contact form next to the assistant. A visitor with an urgent or unusual need should not be trapped in an automated loop.

Check privacy and access

Document what visitors may submit, where conversations are stored, who can review them, how long data is retained, and how the business can remove access.

Price the whole year

Include the plan, usage limits, setup time, source maintenance, human review, support, renewal, cancellation, and the cost of correcting a wrong answer.

Prove the outcome

Track qualified inquiries, successful handoffs, resolved questions, and operating cost. Chat volume alone is activity, not proof that the business earned more.

Decision rule: recommend an assistant only after a real-site test passes the source, boundary, handoff, privacy, ownership, and cost checks. Read the provider’s current feature information separately from the buying decision.
