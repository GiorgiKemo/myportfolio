After-launch support guide

A website is not finished when it goes live.

Small businesses usually need a dependable way to fix issues, update content, protect ownership, and understand what visitors are doing. Maintenance should be specific work with a clear boundary—not an open-ended promise.

Service disclosure: this page describes direct website support that Giorgi may be able to provide. It is not an affiliate recommendation, and it does not promise a particular response time, ranking, conversion rate, or business result. Confirm scope, availability, and pricing before work begins.

Need a second opinion on your site? Describe the issue and request a website review. Include the site URL, the problem, urgency, and what outcome you need.

Fixes and reliability

Investigate broken forms, layout regressions, console errors, failed integrations, slow pages, and deployment problems with a reproducible checklist.

Migration and ownership

Document the domain, hosting, repository, analytics, forms, DNS, billing, and account owners before moving a site or changing a provider.

Performance and accessibility

Review mobile layout, loading cost, image handling, headings, labels, keyboard focus, contrast, and the experience on the actual devices customers use.

SEO and analytics hygiene

Check indexability, canonicals, sitemap, redirects, visible content, structured data, consent, and meaningful events. A dashboard number is not automatically a business result.

Content and conversion updates

Keep services, prices, contact paths, policies, proof, and calls to action current. Test the handoff after every meaningful change.

Clear handover

Leave the business with the files, accounts, instructions, change record, and known limitations it needs to operate without unnecessary lock-in.

Good maintenance starts with a bounded problem. A focused audit, migration, performance pass, or content update is easier to estimate than “make the site better.” Request a scoped conversation if you already know the first task.
