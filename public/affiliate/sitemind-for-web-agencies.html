Add useful AI support to a client site without adding a new mess.

A grounded website assistant can be a useful add-on for a web build, but the commercial model matters. Decide whether the client should own the account or whether your agency will package the assistant into an ongoing service.

Referral partner

Use this when the client should own billing, support, and the assistant account. You make the introduction, keep the implementation boundary clear, and avoid becoming the client’s support desk by accident.

Agency or reseller setup

Use this when your agency already sells website maintenance or marketing retainers. Confirm the white-label, account-access, invoicing, and support rules before bundling it into a recurring offer.

Sell the outcome, not “an AI chatbot”

Define the first job: answering documented service questions, qualifying an inquiry, routing a support request, or helping visitors find a policy. Avoid promising autonomous expertise.

Choose who owns the source material

Assign responsibility for prices, hours, services, policies, and stale pages. The assistant can only be as reliable as the content someone is willing to review.

Keep the handoff visible

Give visitors a real contact route and tell the assistant when to stop. Test sensitive, urgent, and undocumented questions before the client invites traffic.

Write the commercial boundary

Put setup, source maintenance, human review, analytics, usage limits, renewals, cancellation, and support ownership into the proposal. “Included” should mean something specific.

Verify the exit path

Document account ownership, export or removal steps, domain control, access changes, and what remains on the site if the client ends the service or leaves your agency.
