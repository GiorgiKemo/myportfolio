AI assistant or live chat? Start with the handoff.

The right choice depends less on the novelty of the tool and more on the questions visitors ask, the sources you can trust, and who takes over when the answer matters.

Sort the questions before choosing the channel

AI assistants fit repeatable, documented questions such as hours, services, delivery areas, and policies. Human chat is stronger when the visitor needs judgment, negotiation, empathy, or a bespoke answer.

Set the source boundary

List the pages, documents, and policies the assistant may use. A confident answer outside the approved material is a liability, so define what happens when the source is missing or stale.

Design the handoff before the launch

Show visitors how to reach a person, when that person is available, and what the assistant must never handle alone. Test an uncertain question and an urgent request before inviting real traffic.

Compare total operating cost

Count setup, source maintenance, conversation limits, human review, integrations, renewal, and cancellation—not just the headline monthly price. A cheap tool that needs constant correction may cost more.

Review privacy and ownership

Document what visitors can submit, where it is stored, who can access it, how long it is retained, who owns the account, and what remains if the tool is removed.

Measure the outcome that pays

Track qualified inquiries, resolved questions, successful handoffs, booked calls, and support cost. Chat volume alone is activity, not proof that the channel helped the business.

Choose AI first

When the site has stable public information, the team needs after-hours coverage, and a clear escalation route exists.

Choose human chat first

When questions are sensitive, high-stakes, highly variable, or dependent on real-time judgment.

Choose a hybrid

When AI can handle the documented first response and a person can take over with the full context.
