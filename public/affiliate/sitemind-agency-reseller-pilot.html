Agency pilot plan

Turn one useful assistant into a service you can actually support.

A white-label website assistant can fit inside a maintenance or marketing retainer—but only if the source content, handoff, billing, and support boundary are clear before the first client goes live.

1. Start with one low-risk site

Choose a business with stable public content and a clear human contact route. Avoid medical, legal, financial, or emergency claims until the provider’s safeguards and your review process are documented.

2. Define the assistant’s job

Pick a narrow first outcome: answer documented service questions, route inquiries, or explain a policy. A smaller promise is easier to test and renew.

3. Price the work you own

Separate setup, source-content review, analytics, ongoing updates, and human support. Your retainer should cover the work around the tool, not just the software seat.

4. Test the failure path

Ask about missing information, stale pricing, sensitive topics, and urgent requests. Make sure the assistant hands the visitor to a person instead of inventing an answer.

5. Write the exit clause

Document account ownership, data removal, access changes, domain control, and what remains on the client site if the retainer ends.
